# NOTES.md

## Design notes

These are the layout decisions behind `entry.png`, the cream one-page edition of
WORLD NEWS IN ONE DAY for Thursday 19 November 2026, and the reasoning for each.
The page carries 40 stories from around the world, supported by a stat band and
region tally, with a date-check footer confirming that the coverage belongs to
that day's news cycle.

- **Cream paper.** The ground is warm newsprint cream rather than digital white
  because the page is arguing, at a glance, that it is a newspaper: one physical
  sheet holding one day. Pure white reads as a web dashboard; cream reads as
  print, and it lets the red, purple, and ochre card tabs sit on top without
  vibrating.

- **The red date tab.** The solid red block holding "19 / NOV 2026" in the
  top-left corner is the anchor of the whole page. It is the first thing the eye
  lands on even at thumbnail size, it visually seeds the one-line promise under
  the masthead ("everything on this page happened or was first reported today"),
  and its red is reused by the date-check footer so the sheet's two "this is
  today" statements bracket the page from both ends.

- **The eight-cell numbers strip.** "The day in numbers" is eight cells, one
  figure each - the 9-6 ICJ vote, the $81bn IPO, the $2tn valuation, the M5.7
  quake, 3.6% UK inflation, the EUR45bn loan, and the two Senate tallies. Eight
  was chosen because it is exactly one row at this page width: the reader gets
  the magnitude of the day in a single horizontal sweep, mixing courts, markets,
  earth, and politics, before reading a single headline.

- **Region colors.** Every card carries a colored tab keyed to one of the seven
  regions in the header bar (Asia purple, Europe blue, Africa ochre, US red,
  Oceania green, Middle East brown). The colors let a reader trace any one
  region's day down the page, and the header bar doubles as a legend plus a
  count of stories per region.

- **Ranking into hero / secondary / tertiary / briefs.** Stories were ranked by
  blast radius, then by whether the day would read as incomplete without them.
  The three hero cards (Shenzhen peace declaration, the ICJ genocide ruling, the
  $81bn IPO) are the stories that change the world's situation, not just one
  country's week, so they get full headlines plus summaries. The five secondary
  cards are major but bounded - a quake, an FOMC meeting, a chip ban - important
  on their own without summaries. The seven tertiary cards are narrower or
  still-developing (Sudan army, Pellola certification, the measles loss) and get
  headline-only cards. The twenty-four evening briefs are everything else the
  day genuinely contained, compressed to one region-tagged line each so breadth
  never costs the top stories their space.

- **The date-check footer.** The contest asked for the news of Thursday
  19 November 2026 specifically, and wire pages habitually carry two-day-old
  material. The footer exists to certify the sheet against exactly that failure:
  it names the kill list (stories checked and rejected as not-Thursday), states
  that every story happened or was first reported on 19 November 2026, and
  attributes sources with disputed claims attributed rather than asserted. It is
  the page auditing itself, in red, at the bottom, mirroring the red date tab at
  the top.
